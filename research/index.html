research

My research lies at the crossroads of symbolic and neural artificial intelligence, with a focus on extracting, representing and exploiting knowledge through knowledge graphs, natural language processing and machine learning.

See also: publications, projects, events, talks.

Research axes

Knowledge Graphs and GNNs

From the characterisation of semantic relations to graph neural networks.

  • Extraction and classification of semantic relations (SemEval-2018 Task 7, EMNLP 2017, IDA 2016)
  • Construction of large-scale knowledge graphs (AI-KG, ISWC 2020; CS-KG, ISWC 2022)
  • Geometric constraints in GNNs, such as edge curvature and Delaunay triangulation, against over-smoothing and over-squashing (ICML 2024; Data & Knowledge Engineering 2025)

Open science and automatic access to scientific literature

  • Construction and completion of scientific knowledge graphs (AI-KG, CS-KG; SCICERO, Knowledge-Based Systems 2022; research hypothesis generation, Knowledge-Based Systems 2025)
  • Automatic indexing of scientific documents (NLDB 2022)
  • Citation prediction with transformers (Information Processing & Management 2024)

Reliability and efficiency of large language models

  • Detection of AI-generated text in scientific publications (LREC 2022; NLDB 2023)
  • Predicting and localising memorization in fine-tuned LLMs (ECAI 2025; AAAI 2026)
  • Multilingual factual hallucination detection (MUCH benchmark, LREC 2026)
  • Interpretability of LLM classifiers with sparse autoencoders (Findings of EACL 2026)
  • Computational efficiency of vision-language models (PACT, CVPR 2025)

Analysis of online content: figurative language, opinions and disinformation

  • Detection of figurative language, irony and humour (Data & Knowledge Engineering 2012; IEEE Computational Intelligence Magazine 2019)
  • Identification of malicious online behaviour: bots, content spammers, trolls (EJASA 2020; TexTrolls; ECNLP 2024)
  • Classification of multimodal hateful content (CLEF 2024)
